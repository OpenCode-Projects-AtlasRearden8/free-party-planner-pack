Menu & Shopping Grid

Between “what will we eat” and “what do we buy” lives one tidy pair of pages. Page 2 holds the menu the way it will actually happen — who's making it, when it's served, what needs the oven. Page 3 is the store, sorted by the aisles you'll walk.

How to use it

  1. Fill the menu: dish, serve-time, whose kitchen it lives in (potluck names count — they're who you'll chase politely on week one).
  2. Use the Needs column as your reality check: fridge space, oven slots, ice. Four dishes needing 430°F at once is a discovery best made on paper.
  3. Mark anything made ahead — cooked today, reheated Saturday. Next-week you will write you a thank-you note.
  4. Build the shopping grid from the menu, not from memory, and shop by the aisle rows. The Got boxes stop double-buying.
  5. Buy for your headcount plus 10%. Leftovers going home with guests is a cheaper problem to have than a sad buffet.

What's inside

  • Page 2 — the menu: dishes, kitchens, serve-times, equipment needs.
  • Page 3 — the shopping grid: aisle by aisle, with Got-ticks. Print this page twice if your list runs long.

A kind note: five dishes done warmly beat twelve done breathlessly. You're hosting people, not a restaurant.

Print at 100% (“Actual size”), US Letter, portrait. Prints nicely in black & white. Personal use: print as many copies as you like.
