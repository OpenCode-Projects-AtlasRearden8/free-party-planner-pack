Day-of Run of Show

Every good party keeps a shape in its back pocket: what warms up, when people arrive, when the food lands, what sends everyone home smiling. One card, taped where day-of-you will actually stand, so the plan lives on paper instead of in a head that already has its hands full.

How to use it

  1. Fill the times in pencil during Week 1 of the countdown. Parties move; the card bends.
  2. Keep it one page. If it doesn't fit on one page, it isn't the run of show — it's the whole party trying to be witnessed twice.
  3. The Who column is where guests get handed a job: “you're on drinks” is a compliment. Nobody wants to be decorative in your kitchen.
  4. Tick rows as you go. The ticking is the treat, and page 3 collects the wobbles plan-B's so the plan stays loose.
  5. Skip a row entirely if the day runs differently. The card has never once told on anyone.

What's inside

  • Page 2 — the card itself: a loose time line plus the party's arc in five beats.
  • Page 3 — the pockets: first-hour jobs, sideways moments, and a good-bye plan.

A kind note: the run of show serves the people, not the other way around. Skip a row and no one will ever know.

The loose shape (fill times as fits)

  • Warm structures before anyone arrives — oven on, lights warm, music low.
  • Welcome first, drinks always — give people something to hold in their hands.
  • Food lands mid-party — the middle is where people are hungry, not the front.
  • One toast or one game — a beat, not a program.
  • Announce an ending — “we wrap up around ” makes goodbyes easy.

Day-of Pockets

Small plans for small wobbles, so the big day stays big-hearted.

First-hour jobs (before the doorbell)

  • Trash emptied and out of sight — the least festive chore, done invisible.
  • Bathroom stocked: paper, hand towel, nothing embarrassing at eye level.
  • Drinks self-serve — a visible station is freedom for everyone including you.
  • Music low enough to talk over; talking is the whole show.
  • Eat something real. The host plate is not a rumor.

When things go sideways

  • Food runs late: (crackers on the counter buy an hour of grace)
  • More people than chairs: (standing crowds are the festive ones)
  • Conversation stalls: (louder music, then a raise-your-glass toast)
